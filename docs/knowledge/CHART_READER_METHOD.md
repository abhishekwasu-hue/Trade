1. PRINCIPLES
 P1 Logic, not mathematics. A trader compares; he does not calculate. Reading rules use comparisons (larger than, beyond, inside, smaller than the others, the same structure) and never fixed numbers, ratios, counts, widths or multipliers. Numbers belong only to operational dashboard settings (lots, SL, target, exits, schedules) and to data-quality checks.
 P2 One continuous chart. The chart is read as one unit, the way a person looks at it. No reading step starts, ends, resets or splits because of a day, a session, a close of trading, a gap or a clock time. A gap is simply the next candle.
 P3 Story first. Read candles together with volume, step by step: who drove each leg, how price arrived, what happened inside the area, who took control. A verdict without the story is invalid.
 P4 Trend side only, pullback entries only. Trade in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend, at an area, after the pullback shows weakness. Never a breakout entry, never a chase, never a counter-trend entry.
 P5 Patience, no prediction. Nobody knows where a correction ends; it can extend. Do not predict the end of a wave and do not block on wave count or maturity. Arm every valid area ahead in the trend direction and read each one when price arrives. If an area fails, the next one is already armed.
 P6 Areas are references until price proves them. An area is not confirmed by being touched and not killed by being entered or poked. It is confirmed only by what price does inside it, and it dies only by structure.
 P7 Probabilistic. A valid read can lose. Never change a rule to turn a losing case into a win; check only that the read followed the method.
 P8 Clean charts. Draw only what is significant and tradeable. Fewer, truer lines and areas. (Display only — the reader reads every
    line.) Shown: the evidence lines (the line in confluence with the entry area that price touched in this visit; for E4 (b) the broken
    line being retested), the channel price is in now, the next line in price's path above and below, and the higher-degree lines price
    respects in the current move. Hidden behind "Show all lines": dead lines, lines outside the trade's path, a lower-degree line nearly
    parallel and close to a higher-degree one, and lines that ended before the current leg's start swing. The number shown is the
    display setting chart_max_lines. Areas: the refined base, the target and the invalidation strong; the context area faint.
 P9 Settings are Abhi's. R:R, SL, target, lots and exits come from the dashboard. The reader never filters on them.

2. READING ORDER (top-down, every read)
 a) Weekly: the overall market state.
 b) Daily: the trend (degree-aware Dow structure). A correction in progress does not change the trend; the trend stays that of the impulse before it.
 c) 1H: the buyer and seller areas (where the next reaction is likely).
 d) 15M: the pullback into the area and the behaviour inside it.
 e) Lower timeframe (5M): the change of character inside the area.
 f) Volume from futures (the index has none); structure, areas and RSI from the spot chart.
 g) Context lines (market sentiment, flows, global cues) are reported as context, never as a trigger.

3. DEFINITIONS
 Structure
 - Swing: a turning point that the following structure respects. Price leaves it by a leg larger than the pullbacks inside that leg. Small wiggles inside a leg are not swings.
 - Degree: swings belong to a structure. A higher-degree swing contains lower-degree swings. Every comparison uses swings of the same degree.
 - Calibration (Abhi, layer 1): a swing's significance is always judged against the market's own normal movement. The approved swing engine (swings2 D1 / D2) does exactly that, at the scale Abhi chose from his own charts. That scale is his visual calibration (a setting), not a fixed point value; his approved swing charts are its acceptance.
 - Impulse and correction: an impulse moves with larger, directional candles and little overlap. A correction moves with overlapping, smaller candles, in recognisable shapes (zigzag, flat, triangle, combinations; channels, wedges and flags are correction shapes too). The anatomy and the structure are read together.
 - Equal: a later extreme that does not go beyond the earlier one (double / triple top or bottom).
 - Big / dominant: larger than the other candles of the same visit or leg. Dominant = larger than every other candle in that context.
 Areas
 - An area is built from ONE base or reaction: the candles where buyers or sellers acted (wick to body). Never join separate bases or timeframes into one area. A higher-timeframe area at the same place is labelled confluence; the trade area is the lower-timeframe one.
 - Kinds: demand, supply, a flipped area (old support now resistance, or the reverse), the origin of a sharp fall or sharp rise.
 - The origin of a break of structure is the base the impulse left — the candle where buyers or sellers acted before the breakout —
   never the breakout candle itself. Its band is wick to body; no wick on the area side ⇒ the body (never the whole candle).
 - Lines: a trendline or a horizontal level is drawn from two separate reactions; price left it between them. The third touch is the trade location. A line is valid only when it follows the structure's own swings. Where a line and an area meet, that is the strongest location (confluence). They also meet when a reaction rises out of (falls out of) the area and turns at the line: the turn is the closest approach to the line since its previous touch, no candle closes beyond the line, and the move then goes away from it; that turn is a touch and the area is labelled "confluence: line". Touches are counted by reactions, not contacts: contacts with no real opposite swing of the line's structure between them are one touch. From the third touch on, every touch is a trade location while the line is unbroken; the touch number is information. A reaction's touch point is its own turn (the swing it forms at the line); a later turn that stays away from the line does not remove the confluence label for that visit.
 Area lifecycle
 - Visit: begins when price enters the area. It ends only by structure: price moves away and builds a swing away from the area, or the area breaks or flips, or a commitment fails and closes outside (then the move is gone; no chasing). Candles that close just outside and come back are the same visit. Time never ends a visit.
 - Sweep: price pokes beyond the area and returns inside before any swing forms beyond it. The area keeps its role.
 - Broken: a close beyond the area and a swing formed beyond it. A broken area is not a trade location yet; wait.
 - Flipped: price went beyond the area and also beyond the real swing on the far side (the last swing of the same structure beyond the area; a wick that only pokes past is a sweep, not that swing). When price is making new extremes and no such swing exists, the far-side swing is the first swing formed after the break; the flip happens when that swing is formed. The area's role reverses. When price comes back from that swing into the flipped area, that move is the pullback and the area is a trade location.
 - Dead: an area flips only once. A flipped area that is broken again (a close beyond it and a swing formed beyond it, as Broken
   above) is dead at once: no second flip; it is never armed again, and no E4 (b) retest may use it. (Abhi, C lifecycle; the earlier
   reading — dead only past that break's far-side swing — stays only as the what-if `flip_dead_rule = far_swing`.)
 Inside the area (the zone study)
 - Handover, slow form: across the visit, the candles of the trend side grow while the opposite side's candles shrink, and volume tilts to the trend side. Patterns that form inside the area (channel, wedge, double / triple top or bottom, sideways base) show who is entering.
 - Handover, fast form: a push into the area is rejected (a long wick on the push side, close back) and is at once taken back by a dominant opposite candle that closes beyond the push candle's body.
 - Opposite side disappearing: when no opposite-side candle appears in the visit, that side is exhausted. That counts in favour of the handover.
 - Absorption: the opposite side keeps coming in small candles and price does not move away (the sideways base): the area is being absorbed. No trade; expect the area to break and watch the next one.
 - A handover, once seen, holds until the commitment within the same visit. It is cancelled by later absorption or by the area breaking.
 Commitment and confirmation
 - Commitment candle: the dominant candle in the trade direction after the handover. Any recognised reversal form qualifies (engulfing, pin, strong close, star, tweezer, and so on, described by comparing body and wicks, never by ratios). Its volume is higher than the average of the candles it is compared with: the candles of the leg that brought price into the area and the opposite-side candles of the visit. The rejected push and the commitment itself are not part of that comparison. A pullback may start on heavy volume, so the largest candle never decides.
 - Lower-timeframe change of character: on the lower timeframe, the structure that was going against the trade breaks by a close, after price entered the area. If the lower timeframe is still trending against the trade, there is no entry.
 - A correction's own line (flag, wedge or channel edge) closed through by the commitment candle AT the area is a valid trigger form. A line break away from an area, or without a commitment, is noise.
 Volume
 - Read volume bars the way the eye reads the pane: is selling (buying) volume growing inside the area or fading? Is the commitment's volume larger than the candles it is compared with (see Commitment)? Raw bars; no time-slot baselines.
 - A pullback may start on rising volume; that is normal. The approach does not decide; the behaviour inside the area does.
 - If the futures volume is missing, that is a data problem: report it and do not enter.
 RSI divergence (the only indicator)
 - Compare consecutive real swings of price with RSI at those swings.
 - Bearish: price makes a higher high while RSI does not go above its previous high (an equal RSI high counts).
 - Bullish (mirror): price makes a lower low while RSI does not go below its previous low (an equal RSI low counts).
 - It is evidence inside the area, never a trigger alone. Its absence is reported too.

4. THE DECISION
 - Watch: every valid area ahead of price in the trend direction is armed, nearest first.
 - Enter: at a visit to an armed area, ALL of these must hold:
  1. the trend side is correct;
  2. a handover is present (slow or fast form);
  3. a commitment candle with its volume comparison passes;
  4. a lower-timeframe change of character, after entry into the area, is present.
  Confirming evidence (selling / buying volume growing inside the area, RSI divergence, confluence, a correction line closed through) is listed, not scored.
 - Wait: the visit is still forming, or the handover is not yet clear.
 - No trade: absorption, a commitment that fails, a lower timeframe still against the trade, a broken-not-flipped area, a breakout away from an area, or a counter-trend side.
 - Afterwards: if the area breaks, the next area is already armed. If the visit ended without a trade, the next visit to the same area is read fresh.
 - Mirror everything for the other side (demand / bull put ↔ supply / bear call).

5. OUTPUT OF EVERY READ
 - The story (a few lines): the trend; where the area came from; how price arrived; what happened inside; who took control; the commitment and its volume comparison; the lower-timeframe change; the RSI view.
 - The verdict: enter / wait / no trade, with the reason in plain words.
 - The evidence list (present / absent). No scores, weights or grades from sums. If a label is needed: A = the required set plus at least one confirming piece; B = the required set only.
 - Every price in the output must exist in the data.

6. NEVER IN THE CODE
 - Numeric thresholds in reading.
 - Time-based reading logic or day resets.
 - Predicting where a wave ends, maturity or wave-count gates.
 - R:R gates.
 - Breakout entries.
 - Bands merged or widened by numbers.
 - Rules fitted to one case.
 - Hard-coded dates or instrument numbers.

8. PA STANDARD CONCEPTS (Abhi 21 Oct) — what each one reads, where it is written down
 Every concept below is EVIDENCE or STATE, read with the bars known at that bar only. None of them enters, blocks or grades a trade on
 its own; the entry is the entry models (last item). Each track document has the full definition, its states, its evidence wording and
 what it is not (docs/knowledge/pa_standard/).
 - Daily leg nature (T1, t1_daily_leg_read.md, t1_legs_compare.md): a leg is an impulse or a correction by comparison with its
   neighbouring legs of the same degree (bodies, overlap, volume per bar); no wave count and no ratio decides it (`daily_leg_read =
   compare`; `elliott` = the old reading, what-if only).
 - Market state per timeframe (T1, t1_mtf_state.md): Weekly to 5M in one vocabulary (trend up / down, range, transition), each from its
   own swings.
 - Area base types and departure (T2, t2_area_types.md): how price came into and left the base (reversal or continuation base) and how
   strongly it departed, compared with the candles before it.
 - Confluence (T2, t2_confluence.md): higher-timeframe levels, lines and Fibonacci retracements are labels where they land ON an area or
   resting liquidity; Fibonacci is never read alone (its ratios are the tool's definition, `pa_fib_ratios`, a declared exception).
 - Liquidity (T2, t2_liquidity.md): obvious swing extremes not yet traded beyond, and inducement (a minor extreme taken just before the
   area).
 - Chart patterns (T4, t4_chart_patterns.md): patterns from the structure swings; a break is real only by a close beyond and a swing
   beyond, otherwise a fakeout; a pattern is context and a retest level, never a breakout entry.
 - Candle forms (T5, t5_candle_forms.md): a form is read against its neighbouring candles and counts only at a location (area, level
   or line).
 - Volume reads (T5, t5_volume_read.md): leg against leg and candle against its neighbours, per-bar averages of the futures volume;
   missing futures volume is a data problem.
 - Hidden divergence (T6, t6_rsi_hidden_divergence.md): in a pullback of a trend, price holds a higher low (lower high) while RSI makes
   a lower low (higher high), read the classic way against the trend's previous swing; continuation evidence only.
 - Range edges (T7, t7_range_edges.md): in a range the edges are the areas (bull put at the lower edge, bear call at the upper edge),
   the range itself read from the structure of that timeframe.
 - Output objects (T8, t8_output_objects.md): one list of drawn objects shared by the approval chart and the review charts, and a
   short story per timeframe; display only.
 - Entry models (E1 aggressive, E2 confirmation, E3 structure, E4 sweep-retest): armed while price visits a valid trade-side area;
   each fires at most once per visit. E2 and E3 never fire while the zone study reads absorption without a handover. Overlapping
   areas of the same role visited together count as one place: one signal per model per visit — a counter only, never one area (see
   the Z-1u bullet below: edges, trigger and invalidation come from the area price is visiting). The dashboard's existing limit of trades per zone applies to these entries as it does to every other entry path.
 - Trigger at the refined base (Abhi 22 Oct, correcting the 21 Oct wording; §3 models / C lifecycle): the trigger candle ITSELF must
   trade INTO the 15M refined base — its range overlaps the base band — for E1 / E2 / E3 / E4 (a); touching only the wider context
   area is not enough (the refined base exists so entry and invalidation come from the narrow base; a candle that never touches it
   would bring back the wide invalidation). With `entry_area_refine = area` (what-if) the visited area plays the base's role. E4 (b):
   the rejection form trades at the retested object and closes on its trade side. A close outside the area on the trade-direction
   side is the confirmation and is allowed (a commitment / rejection candle trades into the base and closes away from it). Price must
   never be beyond the INVALIDATION side: no close beyond the area's far edge or the model's invalidation, and (E1) the refined base
   not already closed through in the visit. A candle that gaps or closes through the invalidation side is the area being broken
   (lifecycle), not an entry. (The 22 Oct first reading — the base only reached somewhere in the visit — is superseded; the 21 Oct
   wording — the close inside the area — stays as the what-if `entry_trigger_rule = inside`, and `legacy` as before.)
 - Sweep / fakeout inside a visit (Abhi 22 Oct; C lifecycle, §3 models): a close beyond the area WITHOUT a swing formed beyond, then
   back inside, is a sweep — the area stays alive and the visit continues. E4 (a) is its model (the next push fails inside the sweep
   extreme; invalidation beyond the sweep extreme); E2 / E3 may still fire later in the same visit (the trigger candle trading into the
   refined base) with an invalidation that includes the sweep extreme; E1 does not come back (the first touch, once per visit). A close
   beyond + a swing formed beyond is the area broken (flip); a later return from the other side is the retest of the flipped area ⇒
   E4 (b). A single close beyond the refined base ends only E1 and is not by itself a break of the area; E2 / E3 / E4 are each judged
   against their own invalidation at their own trigger.
 - Trades per zone: the dashboard `max_hits_per_zone` is 4 (Abhi 22 Oct: up to four model entries per visit); a dashboard setting.
 - Overlapping areas are never one area (Z-1u): the cluster only counts one signal per model per place. The area's edges, the trigger
   check and the invalidation always come from the actual area price is visiting, never from the union of the cluster.
 - Refined base (RESEARCH DEFAULT — review after the replay): the 1H / Daily area is the context (where). Inside it, the refined base on
   the setup timeframe (15M) by the same base rule: the newest 15M turn of the area's side inside the band (the swing candle's wick to
   body), from the area's own birth turn on. It gives the trigger location (the trigger candle itself trades into it, Abhi 22 Oct),
   the E1 entry (the visit's first candle at the refined base) and the invalidation (E1: beyond the base's far side; E2 / E3: beyond the farther of the
   base's far side and the visit's extreme). No refined base yet: the models wait.
 - E4 (b) revisit: a broken line or area is being revisited only while price is actually back at it; the revisit ends when price leaves
   it by structure (a new swing that does not reach it).
