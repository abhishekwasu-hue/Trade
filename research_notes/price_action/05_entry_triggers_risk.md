# 05 — Entry triggers, commitment candles, false breakouts, stops and targets (NIFTY 15m credit-spread seller)

Scope: extends, does not repeat, `Candlestick patterns आणि मानसशास्त्र/` (Bulkowski per-pattern tables, academic candlestick evidence, TA-Lib definitions, the bot's composite rejection score), `trendline_rules_reference.md` (Bulkowski trendline stats, Sperandeo 1-2-3), `Chart knowledge/areas_liquidity_fib_channels.md` §2 (sweep rules, Osler stop-cluster evidence) and `Chart knowledge/session_expiry_mtf_theta.md` §4 (theta, σ-room, probability of touch). Those are referenced, not duplicated.

## 1. Takeaway

1. Every serious framework reviewed (Brooks, Wyckoff, DeMark, SFP/sweep, pin bar, retest) converges on the same three-part trigger: **(a) price goes beyond a reference level, (b) it fails to hold there, and (c) the bar *closes* back on the original side.** The close is the commitment. A wick alone is "a sweep or a breakout attempt", not a signal ([LuxAlgo SFP](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/swing-failure-pattern.md)).
2. Context dominates the candle. Brooks's moderators: "the better the context, the less important the signal bar" ([Brooks forum, tails](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/encyclopedia-of-charts-patterns/how-to-read-tails/)). Bulkowski's best single-candle rates (engulfing 63%, shooting star 59%, hammer 60%) are "near random" on their own, and improve mainly by location and trend ([BullEngulfing](https://www.thepatternsite.com/BullEngulfing.html), [ShootingStar](https://www.thepatternsite.com/ShootingStar.html)).
3. Brooks's own probability numbers for the user's situation: a with-trend pullback entry is a 40–60% proposition; "60% of the time your trade won't reach a 2R swing profit" ([Brooks forum, reversal distributions](https://brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/reversal-distributions)). Range breakouts fail ~50% on the first attempt and ~80% overall; channel breakouts fail ~70–75% within 5 bars ([Brooks forum, Orlando Q&A](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/orlando-workshop-2025-qa/clarification-on-a-few-rules/)). These support "enter on the failed break of the pullback extreme, not on the break".
4. **Confirmation costs more than it earns in trend-pullback buying.** The only quantitative comparison found (Nasdaq-100, 25 yr, trend-filtered, RSI zone entries) shows anticipation beat confirmation in 17 of 18 zones: 56.3% vs 53.2% win rate, PF 1.38 vs 1.16 ([Backtest Substack](https://backtest.substack.com/p/why-waiting-for-rsi-confirmation)). Brooks's forum says the same qualitatively: waiting for a second entry gives "higher probability at the cost of ... missing opportunities" ([Brooks forum, stops](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/stop-1-tick-below-get-triggered/)). For an option *seller*, the trade-off tilts back toward confirmation, because the seller's edge is theta, not the first 50 points of the move. [INF]
5. Stops: every framework places the stop **just beyond the extreme of the failure** (signal bar, sweep wick, spring low, broken level). Brooks's one-tick stop "happens quite often" to get hit in channels/trading ranges; the fixes offered are a second entry, a wider swing stop, or re-entry at half size. Generic stop-loss rules add nothing on average (Twiggs ANOVA: all stop variants "achieved less return, and were riskier"), but they cut tails in momentum regimes (Han-Zhou-Zhu: max monthly loss −49.8% → −11.4% at a 10% stop). For a 2-day defined-risk spread, the stop is a *thesis-invalidation* exit, not a volatility filter. [INF]
6. Targets: Brooks's measured move is a soft target (60% claimed, ~50–52% by a student's backtest). Bulkowski's trendline measure rule uses 56–63% of the widest gap (already in `trendline_rules_reference.md`). For the seller, the target is premium capture, so the chart target only needs to say *where the short strike can sit* and *how far the pullback can go before the thesis fails*.
7. Option-seller management: the one peer-of-record style study found (OptionAlpha, SPY bull put, 30 DTE, 0.30/0.10 delta, 5 yr) shows that a 50% profit target plus a 25% stop cut max loss from $12,000 to $2,000 and raised Sharpe 0.62 → 0.77 while lowering raw return; a 75% target did better than 50% (PF 1.34 vs 1.26); stop losses in general "typically underperformed" ([OptionAlpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/spy-put-credit-spread-backtest)). This supports Abhi's 60–80% capture target and argues for a *chart-based* exit over a *P&L-based* stop.

## 2. Cited findings

### 2a. Al Brooks: signal bar, entry bar, second entry, failed breakouts, measured move

- **[RULE] Signal bar quality (bull):** close in upper 25–33% of range, small upper tail, open near low, range larger than recent bars, trades above prior bar's high; bear is the mirror. Already in `algorithmic_definitions.md` §4 — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/signal-bars-entry-bars-price-action).
- **[RULE] Entry = stop order one tick beyond the signal bar; protective stop one tick beyond the other end.** "I set my stop 1 tick below/above my signal bar as Al suggested." Moderator: being stopped then seeing the move go "happens quite often in reversals with a lot of TR PA (= channels)". Remedies offered: re-enter ("the right thing to do"), "reduce your position size in half", "use the wide stop", or "wait for the second entry". Trade-off stated: "You will increase your chances of winning at the price of missing opportunities that will take off without you." — [Brooks forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/stop-1-tick-below-get-triggered/)
- **[PRAC] Tails:** a tail is weakness but not a disqualifier; "if the context is good you should still take the trade"; "the better the context, the less important the signal bar". Waiting for a follow-through bar is optional: higher probability, higher risk (wider stop). Student rule of skipping bars with tails > 1/3 of range was neither endorsed nor rejected. — [Brooks forum, tails](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/encyclopedia-of-charts-patterns/how-to-read-tails/)
- **[RULE] Second entry (H2/L2):** a buy signal after a trendline break is H1; if that fails and the correction continues, the next buy signal "after two trend line breaks ... is what Brooks calls a H2 buy"; it "ends a two leg corrective move". "If you want high probability trades then second entry is safer ... you have to give up some profit for that safety." A 1–2 bar correction followed by a new signal is "a minor" second signal. — [EliteTrader thread](https://elitetrader.com/et/threads/what-is-a-second-entry.376793/) (secondary source; no primary Brooks text).
- **[STAT, Brooks course slides as quoted by students]** Reversals: "Only 40 percent of reversals go far enough for a swing trade"; "Only 20 percent get big, opposite trend"; "60% of the time, your trade won't reach a 2R swing profit. You'll either get stopped out or exit with a small profit. 40% of the time, your trade will reach 2R and some will reach much more." — [Brooks forum, reversal distributions](https://brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/reversal-distributions)
- **[STAT, Brooks course as quoted]** Breakouts: "In a Trading Range there's a 50% chance that the 1st Breakout attempt will fail"; "80% of Breakout attempts fail" (inertia: market keeps doing what it has been doing); channel breakouts fail "about 70%", and "within about 5 bars 75% of the time". Moderator: these are "weighted evidence", not precise percentages. — [Brooks forum, Orlando Q&A](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/orlando-workshop-2025-qa/clarification-on-a-few-rules/)
- **[STAT]** Strong breakout → measured move: theory "60% probability of reaching MM target"; a student's backtest/live record says "more 50-52%". Brooks's three breakout outcomes as quoted: ~20% immediate reversal, else channel continuation or deep pullback into a range. "80% of reversal attempts fail." — [Brooks forum, MM probability](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/41-trading-breakouts/bo-has-60-probability-of-reaching-mm-target-but-is-it-really/)
- **[RULE] Trader's equation:** "the probability of success times the reward has to be greater than the probability of failure times your [risk]"; for a 50% trade "the reward needs to be about twice the size of my risk"; he wants ≥ 60% before holding a directional belief. — [MoneyShow transcript](https://moneyshow.com/articles/videotranstr-26221). Forum: "for 90% of the bars in a chart [the probability] to go X ticks up and X ticks down is between 40-60%"; the directional probability "refers to equidistant moves" and must not be mixed with reward:risk math. — [Brooks forum, 40-60](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/30-traders-equation-and-probability/clarifying-the-40-60-rule-possible-contradiction/)
- **[PRAC] Measured move:** projected after "a breakout with follow-through"; "there are two ways to draw the Measured Move" (leg height vs. range/spike height — the forum never settles which); used as a limit-order profit target, and multiple MM targets usually coexist with other S/R. — [Brooks article](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/?p=121544), [MM forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/41-trading-breakouts/bo-has-60-probability-of-reaching-mm-target-but-is-it-really/)
- Gap: no primary text was retrievable for "failed failure" / "five-tick failure"; the forum thread on failed breakouts is unanswered — [Brooks forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/15-breakouts/failed-breakout/).

### 2b. Candlestick statistics not already in the notes (Bulkowski, daily US stocks, 10-day horizon)

- **[STAT] Bullish engulfing:** reversal 63% (rank 22/103 on reversal rate, but **overall performance rank 84/103**); frequency rank 12; best when "within a third of the yearly low"; "Avoid bullish engulfing candles that appear in a downward primary trend"; look "for a downward retrace in a rising price trend". — [BullEngulfing](https://www.thepatternsite.com/BullEngulfing.html)
- **[STAT] Shooting star:** reversal 59%, "near random"; overall rank 55; best 10-day move 3.86% vs his 6% "good" benchmark; best as "part of an upward retracement in a downward price trend"; "do not depend on that happening". — [ShootingStar](https://www.thepatternsite.com/ShootingStar.html)
- **[STAT] Busted patterns:** a breakout that moves ≤ 10% then closes back through the opposite side; single busts 19%, double and triple 1% each (triple tops); busted triple tops avg rise 42% vs 37% from genuine triple bottoms, i.e. a *failed* pattern outperforms the real one. — [Busted](https://www.thepatternsite.com/Busted.html)
- **[STAT] Throwbacks/pullbacks after breakouts** (9,372 patterns, 841 stocks, 1991–2008): partial retrace → 41.3% avg rise vs 26.7% after a full retrace below the breakout price; pullbacks 25.1% vs 17.3%. A stop at the breakout-day low then a volatility stop earned ~11% more than the volatility stop alone (throwbacks only). Rule quoted: "place a close stop below the breakout day's low" and switch to a volatility stop once it rises above. — [ThrowPull](https://www.thepatternsite.com/ThrowPull.html)
- **[STAT] Fidelity "Top 10 candles":** closing location is written into the best patterns' definitions ("all should close near the low", "close near the high"); the top performers are mostly 3-bar patterns with 10-day moves of 5.7–8.8%. — [Fidelity PDF](https://fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/Top10CandlesWork_620095.pdf)
- Academic evidence (Marshall 2006/2008, Lu 2012/2014/2015, Caginalp & Laurent 1998, Duvinage 2013, Tharavanij 2017): see `empirical_evidence.md`. Net: raw patterns ≈ no edge; trend-conditioned, 2–3-day holds, bullish side, flexible exits are where positives cluster. Nothing on 15m index bars.

### 2c. Rejection / "price failure" definitions

- **[RULE] Pin bar:** tail "commonly around two-thirds of the total range or more"; small body at the opposite end; "closed back near where it started or beyond"; "the tail should stick out beyond the adjacent bars or through a nearby level"; entry on "a break of the nose-side extreme"; stop "just beyond the tail's extreme" (some add a buffer because tails attract stop runs); "mid-range pin bars are mostly noise"; "even well-placed pin bars fail regularly". — [LuxAlgo pin bar](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/pin-bar.md)
- **[RULE] Swing failure pattern (SFP):** "a failed raid on a prior swing point"; wick beyond prior swing, close back inside; "A wick beyond the level means nothing until the bar completes"; "The further back inside the close, the cleaner the pattern"; strict = same candle, loose = next candle; follow-through checks = LTF change of character or close through the sweep candle's opposite extreme; stop "just beyond the sweep wick"; target = rotation through the range; "There is no honest fixed number" for win rate; many raids pause and continue. — [LuxAlgo SFP](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/swing-failure-pattern.md)
- **[RULE] Wyckoff spring/upthrust:** undercut of support with little follow-through, recovery inside range; classic = shallow on light volume; terminal shakeout = deeper, heavier volume, acceptable "if the recovery is decisive"; "The confirmation is the test, a quiet pullback holding above the spring low"; test volume must be quieter than the spring's; long "with the stop below the spring"; a close back below the spring low cancels the read; upthrust is the mirror at distribution tops. — [LuxAlgo spring](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/spring.md). StockCharts/Pruden practice: "Purchase ... on the test of the Spring and place a stop below the low of the Spring"; a Spring #2 "can be bought on a test"; after a Sign of Strength expect a Last Point of Support as another entry. — [StockCharts Wyckoff](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2016-04-wyckoff-skill-building/). (The #1/#2/#3 depth-volume taxonomy was not retrievable from a primary source.)
- **[RULE] Retest of a broken level/trendline:** valid only if the level was "closed through decisively rather than merely wicked"; use "a band around the level scaled to recent volatility"; "rejection wicks and a close back in the breakout direction argue the level held"; "a prompt, decisive rejection is the strong form"; price sitting flat on the level weakens it; "a full-bodied close back through it converts the event into a false breakout"; enter on the retest "with a stop just beyond the level"; no fixed bar window ("often within a few bars, but it can take much longer, or never"). — [LuxAlgo retest](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/retest/)
- **[RULE] DeMark TD-line breakout qualifiers** (close-based): Q1 = the bar before the breakout closed *against* the breakout direction (for a down-break, close[X−1] > close[X−2]); Q2 = the breakout bar opens beyond the line and holds; Q3 = a projected "true range" test: close[X−1] minus (max(high, prior close) − close) must still sit beyond the line. "If none of the qualifiers has confirmed a breakout, the signal is considered as false." Target = height of the largest swing above/below the line projected from the break. Exit: if the next bar opens back through the level, exit at the open; if it can't extend beyond the breakout bar's extreme, exit at its close. — [LiteFinance TD lines](https://www.litefinance.org/blog/for-professionals/td-lines-by-tom-demark/) (secondary; the article's Q3 arithmetic is internally inconsistent).

### 2d. Confirmation vs anticipation

- **[STAT] Nasdaq-100 stocks, 25 yr daily, trend filter (20>50>200 SMA, SPY>200), RSI(7) zone entry, next-open fill, 5-day hold.** Buying on the down day ("cross under" = anticipation) vs the up day ("cross over" = confirmation): zone 25–30: 56.25% vs 53.19% win, 0.52% vs 0.24% avg net, PF 1.38 vs 1.16, 3,865 vs 4,189 trades. Zone 15–20: PF 1.20 vs 0.83. Anticipation won in 17 of 18 zones; confirmation PF < 1 in five zones. — [Backtest Substack](https://backtest.substack.com/p/why-waiting-for-rsi-confirmation); mirror write-up [TradingTimeMachine](https://www.tradingtimemachine.com/rsi-backtest.html). Caveats: daily bars, stocks not index, no costs stated, one author.
- **[PRAC] Brooks:** first entry = lower probability / smaller stop / more reward; second entry or follow-through bar = higher probability / wider stop / fewer trades (sources in 2a).
- **[STAT] Bulkowski** measures reversal rates *after* a confirming close beyond the pattern (see `empirical_evidence.md` §2), so his 59–63% rates are already confirmation-conditional.

### 2e. Stops, targets, R:R

- **[STAT] Twiggs (Incredible Charts), EMA-crossover system, ANOVA over no-stop vs 1–10% trailing and 2×ATR(5) stops:** "none of the stop methods tested improved the 'NO STOP LOSS' portfolio's daily mean return"; "all combinations of stop loss tested achieved less return, and were riskier". — [Incredible Charts](https://www.incrediblecharts.com/2011-04-19_whats_new.php)
- **[STAT] Han, Zhou & Zhu, momentum + 10% stop, 1926–2013:** max monthly loss −49.79% → −11.36% (EW) and −64.97% → −23.28% (VW); Sharpe "more than doubled"; Alpha Architect replication: lower drawdown, but simple long-only had better CAGR/Sharpe; costs "might eliminate all the benefits". — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)
- **[STAT] Kaminski & Lo (J. Financial Markets 2014):** "certain stop-loss policies can increase expected return while substantially reducing volatility" for index-futures buy-and-hold at longer sampling frequencies; value depends on return autocorrelation (momentum helps, mean reversion hurts). — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)
- **[RULE] ATR stops:** "mostly falls between about 1.5 for short-term trades and 3 for trend-following"; ATR(14) Wilder; measured on the trading timeframe ("the noise your position must survive bar to bar"); hybrid = "anchor beyond a structural point such as a swing low and add an ATR fraction as buffer"; "Smaller multiples exit sooner but get shaken out more often; larger ones survive noise but give back more." No backtest cited. — [LuxAlgo ATR stop](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/atr-based-stop-distance.md)
- **[RULE] MAE/MFE (Sweeney):** collect ≥ 200 trades with a *wide* stop so MAE is not truncated; plot MAE of winners vs losers; "Place it 1-2 ticks beyond the 95th percentile MAE of your winners"; MFE distribution sets the target and the breakeven trigger ("that level is a statistically grounded trigger for break-even moves"); bar-close data "understate both MAE and MFE". — [NexusFi MAE](https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/maximum-adverse-excursion-mae), [LuxAlgo MAE/MFE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/mae-mfe-informed-management.md)
- **[RULE] Brooks:** 2:1 reward:risk at 40% win = breakeven-plus; 1:1 needs 60%; probabilities computed on *initial* risk at entry ("at the time you put on the trade all probabilities must be based on initial risk"), actual risk only after a pullback. — [Brooks forum, 40-60](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/30-traders-equation-and-probability/clarifying-the-40-60-rule-possible-contradiction/)

### 2f. Option-seller specifics

- **[STAT] OptionAlpha SPY bull put, 30 DTE, short 0.30Δ / long 0.10Δ, 5 yr, no commissions:** hold-to-expiry 93% win (5% allocation), RoC 39% at 10% allocation, Sharpe 0.62, max loss $12,000; add 50% profit target + 25% stop + close at 15 DTE → max loss $2,000, Sharpe 0.77, lower RoC, 295 vs 133 positions; raising the stop to 50% "increased [downside] with no corresponding rise in average profitability"; 75% target: PF 1.34 vs 1.26, Sharpe 0.83; "Positions with stop losses typically underperformed." — [OptionAlpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/spy-put-credit-spread-backtest)

Numbers are in images only; not extractable. — [TalkMarkets summary](https://talkmarkets.com/article/managing-winners-managing-early-and-managing-based-on-theta?post=237791). A retail replication reported ~70% win rate but negative ROI because losers were held to expiry. — [SweetVolatility](https://sweetvolatility.com/tasty-trade-experiments/)

## 3. Commitment-candle: codable composite definition

A **commitment candle** is the first *closed* 15m bar (or N≤3 merged bars, per `algorithmic_definitions.md` §6) that satisfies all gates below. Direction = impulse direction (long example; mirror for short).

| # | Feature | Codable test (scale-free) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Location** | Low of bar (or merged bars) is inside or beyond the buyer area / prior swing low / C-wave target zone (band ± k·ATR). | Bulkowski: location is the main improver (yearly-range third); LuxAlgo: mid-range pins are noise; Brooks: context > bar. |
| G2 | **Excursion beyond reference** | `pen = (level − L)/ATR14 > 0` (touched or swept). Sweep flavour: L < prior swing low. | SFP, spring, pin-bar protrusion rule: tail must "stick out beyond the adjacent bars or through a nearby level". |
| G3 | **Reclaim on close** | `C > level` and `CL = (C−L)/(H−L) ≥ 0.67` (close in top third) **or** `C > H[t−1]` (closes beyond prior bar's high). | SFP: "wick means nothing until the bar completes"; "further back inside the close, the cleaner"; Brooks: close in upper 25–33%; DeMark/Bulkowski: breaks judged on closes. |
| G4 | **Rejection shape** | `lower_wick/range ≥ 0.5` (pin flavour) **or** `body/range ≥ 0.6 with C > O` (engulf/strong-close flavour). Either passes; both = stronger. | Pin bar 2/3 convention; Fidelity top-10 patterns all specify "close near the high/low". |
| G5 | **Relative size** | `range/median20 ≥ 1.0` and not climactic in the *pullback* direction (a wide bar that closes back through the level is strength, not exhaustion). | Brooks: range larger than recent bars; `algorithmic_definitions.md` §6 A2 logic. |
| G6 | **Not a trading-range bar** | overlap with prior 3 bars `< 0.6` of own range, or close beyond prior bar's extreme. | Brooks: one-tick stops fail "in reversals with a lot of TR PA". |
| G7 | **Second-attempt preference (soft)** | Count prior failed reclaim attempts at this zone in the current pullback; 2nd attempt scores higher than 1st (H2/L2). | Brooks second entry; Wyckoff "buy the test, not the spring". |
| G8 | **Not acceptance** | No two consecutive closes beyond the level before this bar (else the level is broken, not swept). | `areas_liquidity_fib_channels.md` §2 acceptance rule; Osler cascade evidence. |
| G9 | **Trend/pullback context** | Higher-TF trend agrees; pullback is a completed corrective structure (ABC/flat/triangle), depth in 38–80% band or at a prior S/D area. | Lu 2014 / Deng 2022 trend conditioning; Bulkowski "retrace in a rising trend"; user's method. |

**Confirmation tier (configurable on dashboard, not hardcoded):**
- Tier 0 (anticipation): enter at the zone touch with no candle. Highest reward, lowest probability; the only quantitative test found favours it for stock pullbacks, but it is *not* the user's method and gives the seller no invalidation point. Not recommended for the bot. [INF]
- Tier 1 (commitment candle): enter at close of the bar that passes G1–G9. Default.
- Tier 2 (follow-through): also require next bar to trade beyond the commitment candle's high (Brooks stop entry) or close in its top half. Use when G6/G7 are weak (trading-range PA, first attempt). Costs opportunities; raises probability (Brooks forum).

**Invalidation of the candle itself:** a *close* below the commitment candle's low (strict) or below the sweep/spring low (looser, Wyckoff) = thesis wrong. This is the chart stop (Section 5).

## 4. Trigger taxonomy

| Trigger | Definition (closed-bar) | Stop | Chart target | Evidence grade | Notes for seller |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rejection / pin** | Long tail beyond level, close in top third, tail protrudes past neighbours. | Beyond tail (+ buffer). | Opposite side of pullback / prior swing. | [PRAC]+Bulkowski ~59–60% (daily). | Weakest alone; needs G1, G9. Common at C-wave end. |
| **Engulf / strong close** | Body closes beyond prior bar's open and extreme; close near high. | Beyond engulfing bar's low. | Same. | Bulkowski 63%, overall rank 84/103. | Better as the *second* bar after a pin (pin + engulf = Brooks "signal bar + entry bar"). |
| **Sweep-and-reclaim (SFP / spring / upthrust)** | Wick beyond prior swing or equal lows, close back inside; optional "test" bar holding above the sweep low. | Beyond sweep wick. | Rotation to other side of range; for a pullback, the impulse high. | [PRAC]; mechanism supported by Osler (stops cluster beyond levels); no hit-rate study. | Best structural fit for "C sweeps A". Beware acceptance: 2 closes beyond = cascade, not sweep. |
| **Line-break + retest** | Price closes decisively through a trendline/level, returns to it from the other side, prints rejection + close in breakout direction. | Just beyond the retested level. | DeMark: height of largest swing projected; Bulkowski: 56–63% of widest gap. | Bulkowski throwback 59% (trendlines); partial-retrace outcomes better than full. | For the seller this is the *pullback into the broken counter-trendline* of the correction — i.e. the correction's own trendline breaks, then retests. |
| **Second entry (H2/L2)** | First reclaim attempt fails (new low), second attempt prints a commitment candle. | Beyond the second signal bar or the deeper low. | Measured move of impulse leg / prior high. | Brooks [PRAC]: higher probability, fewer trades. | Default "upgrade" when first attempt happened in trading-range PA. |
| **Failed breakout of the pullback extreme (busted pattern)** | Pullback breaks its own support (looks like reversal), moves ≤ small amount, then closes back above. | Beyond the bust low. | Bulkowski: busted patterns outperform genuine ones (42% vs 37%). | [STAT] daily stocks. | Same thing as sweep-and-reclaim at larger scale; trapped breakout sellers fuel the move. |

Ranking by expected probability (practitioner consensus, not measured): second entry ≈ sweep-and-reclaim with test > line-break retest > engulf > pin alone. Ranking by reward per unit risk is roughly the reverse. [INF]

## 5. Stops and targets rules

**Stop (thesis invalidation on the underlying, judged on 15m close):**
1. Primary: close below the commitment candle's low (long) / above its high (short). Brooks one-tick analogue, but *close-based* to avoid the "stop 1 tick below got triggered" failure in channels. [RULE + INF]
2. Structural alternative when the candle is small or in overlap: close beyond the sweep/spring low, or beyond the C-wave extreme, plus a buffer of a fraction of ATR (LuxAlgo hybrid). [RULE]
3. Hard invalidation regardless of candle: acceptance beyond the impulse origin (100% retrace) = reversal, exit (per `areas_liquidity_fib_channels.md`). [RULE]
4. Do **not** use a generic ATR or % trailing stop on the spread P&L as the primary exit: Twiggs and OptionAlpha both found such stops reduce return; the momentum-crash evidence supports stops only as tail-cutters. For defined-risk spreads the max loss already caps the tail. [STAT → INF]
5. Calibrate the buffer with MAE: after ≥ 200 bot trades logged with a *wide* stop, set the buffer 1–2 ticks (NIFTY: a few points) beyond the 95th-percentile MAE of winners, measured on intrabar (1m or tick) data, not 15m closes. [RULE]

**Targets (chart) and premium (spread):**
6. Chart targets, in order of preference: (a) prior impulse high / opposite edge of range; (b) measured move = impulse leg height from the pullback low (Brooks: ~50–60% reach); (c) DeMark/Bulkowski line-break projections. These set *where the short strike must not be* and *how much room exists*; they are not profit targets for the seller. [RULE]
7. Short strike sits beyond the stop level (point 1–3), so that the chart stop triggers *before* the strike is threatened; express room in σ (`session_expiry_mtf_theta.md`). Because P(touch) ≈ 2×P(ITM), require the invalidation level, not the strike, to be the first thing price can hit. [INF]
8. Premium target 60–80% of credit, time-boxed ~2 sessions: supported by OptionAlpha (75% target beat 50%; both beat hold) and the √T decay profile of OTM premium. Exit earlier if the chart target (6a) is reached, since the remaining premium is small and gamma near weekly expiry rises. [STAT + INF]
9. R:R ≥ 1:3 is evaluated on the **underlying**: (distance stop→entry) vs (distance entry→chart target). With Brooks's 40–60% directional range, 1:3 on the underlying is the condition that makes a 40% hit rate profitable; the spread then converts the "win" into theta capture rather than the full move. [RULE + INF]
10. Position management for a 2-day hold: (a) after the first follow-through bar closes beyond the commitment candle, move the chart stop to the commitment candle's midpoint (Brooks "actual risk"); (b) breakeven trigger from the MFE distribution once logged (LuxAlgo); (c) time stop: if neither 50% premium nor the chart target is reached by the second session's close, exit — holding into expiry day converts a theta trade into a gamma trade (tastytrade 21-DTE logic scaled to weeklies). [INF]

## 6. Pitfalls

- **Entering on the wick, not the close.** All frameworks: "nothing until the bar completes". The bot must evaluate on closed bars only (lookahead rules in `algorithmic_definitions.md` §5).
- **Confusing sweep with acceptance.** Osler: a break through a stop cluster *accelerates*. Two closes beyond the level is fuel, not a trap.
- **Treating 59–63% candle rates as edge.** They are daily-stock, confirmation-conditional, and "near random" by Bulkowski's own words. The edge, if any, is in G1 + G9.
- **One-tick stops in trading-range price action.** Brooks's forum documents the failure; use close-based or second-entry logic there.
- **Generic P&L stops on spreads.** OptionAlpha: stop-loss variants "typically underperformed"; a 50% stop increased downside without raising profitability. Exit on chart invalidation instead.
- **Over-confirming.** The only quantitative test shows confirmation cost ~3 pp win rate and ~0.2 PF in trend pullbacks; Brooks: fewer trades. Make the confirmation tier a dashboard setting and measure both.
- **Letting the sweep extreme and the short strike coincide.** Stops cluster just beyond obvious lows; the bot's own strike at the obvious pool is the first thing a cascade reaches.
- **Reading Brooks's percentages as data.** His moderators call them "weighted evidence"; one student's backtest cut 60% to 50–52%.
- **Bulkowski/academic numbers are daily and US.** No 15m NIFTY study exists; everything here is a hypothesis to validate walk-forward with same-context random baselines (`algorithmic_definitions.md` §5).
